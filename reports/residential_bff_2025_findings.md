# Targets, self-contained housing and funding — 2025

Four documented award cases are compared against the same annual targets using different measures: official BFF progress; new residential construction only; and new construction plus independent ARUs where a complete breakdown is verified.

The Building Faster Fund (BFF) was Ontario's three-year program of up to C$1.2 billion supporting housing-enabling infrastructure. The numeric funding threshold was 80% of the annual target, not full target achievement.

| Municipality | Annual target | Official BFF progress | Official % | New-construction count used | New-construction % | Announced allocation C$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| London | 4700 | 4224 | 89.87% | 2553 | 54.32% | 11,263,707 |
| Kitchener | 3500 | 3005 | 85.86% | 2516 | 71.89% | 8,013,600 |
| Sarnia | 100 | 654 | 654.00% | 299 | 299.00% | 400,000 |
| Welland | 430 | 528 | 122.79% | 458 | 106.51% | 1,669,317 |

London and Kitchener total C$19,277,307 in announced allocations and fall below 80% in a new-construction-only scenario. This does not establish improper payments or dependence on institutional beds.

**Kitchener provides an important control:** its municipality reports 2,516 starts + 489 ARUs = 3,005 units, or 85.86% of its target. Self-contained homes explain the published total without attributing the difference to institutional beds. The archived December CMHC edition reported 2,416 starts; the 100-unit difference in the later release is preserved as a vintage difference.

Sarnia (299/100) and Welland (458/430) already clear the threshold with conventional new construction in the edition consulted.

**London:** 54.32% from new construction alone versus 89.87% officially. The 4,224 official total still needs a component breakdown separating ARUs/conversions, institutional categories and revisions. Do not claim London qualified only by counting beds.

Independent ARUs and conversions can house families. Excluding them is appropriate only for an explicitly new-construction-only measure. A residential measure that excludes beds should include verified independent ARUs. Starts are not completions, occupancy or affordability.

Only the December 2025 municipal CMHC count is available here. Difference from the award count is not attributed to institutional beds.
- **Kitchener**: [annual target](https://www.kitchener.ca/strategic-plans-and-projects/strategic-plan/2023-2026-strategic-plan-progress/); [result and announced allocation](https://www.kitchener.ca/news/posts/kitchener-earns-8-million-from-the-building-faster-fund-for-third-consecutive-year-of-housing-target-achievement/). Municipal release explicitly decomposes 3,005 into 2,516 housing starts + 489 ARUs. The archived December CMHC edition has 2,416 starts, so the 100-unit vintage difference must not be called beds.
- **Sarnia**: [annual target](https://news.ontario.ca/en/release/1007629/ontario-rewards-sarnia-with-400000-through-the-building-faster-fund); [result and announced allocation](https://news.ontario.ca/en/release/1007629/ontario-rewards-sarnia-with-400000-through-the-building-faster-fund). 299 conventional starts alone already exceed the 80-unit threshold; no claim that institutional additions were necessary to qualify.
- **Welland**: [annual target](https://www.welland.ca/news/posts/city-of-welland-receives-167m-for-exceeding-housing-targets/); [result and announced allocation](https://www.welland.ca/news/posts/city-of-welland-receives-167m-for-exceeding-housing-targets/). 458 conventional starts alone already exceed 100% of the target. The 70-unit gap is not automatically attributed to beds.

Official municipal/provincial facts were reviewed through web research and transcribed into the case registry; this is not automatic document extraction. Registry and residential-data hashes are in merge_audit.json. The four cases do not represent a complete audit of the 18 confirmed recipients and cannot establish a total amount attributable to institutional beds.
